HOA boards and nonprofits often have to collect three bids. Then they sit down with three proposals that look nothing alike and try to figure out which one is the best deal.

My advice: ask every builder to break the price into line items. Hoops, surfacing, fencing, any slab extension, each priced separately. That lets your board compare apples to apples, and it lets you choose what to build now and what to add later.

## Why lump-sum bids are hard to compare

A single total number tells you almost nothing. One bid might include a top rail and round posts. Another might not. One might be a resurface while another is a new post-tension slab. Totals hide all of that.

When we've worked with boards on this, they asked us to itemize so they could put our bid next to the others. That's exactly the right instinct.

## The line items to ask for

Ask every builder to price these separately:

- **Slab work:** new slab, extension, or work over an existing court.
- **Surfacing:** the coating system on top.
- **Fencing:** posts, fabric, top rail, gates, windscreen.
- **Hoops or nets:** basketball systems, net posts, portable nets.
- **Extensions or add-ons:** extra concrete, lighting, shade-ready footers.

Then lay the three bids out in a simple table, one row per item. Gaps jump out right away.

## What to check in each line

The price is only half of it. Here are the questions I'd ask on each line.

**Slab.** Is this a resurface, an unengineered post-tension cap, or a fully engineered slab? Those are three very different projects. If it's engineered, is the soil work included, or is it pending a geotech report? Honest builders will tell you they can't price dirt work until the drilling is done.

**Surfacing.** Is it standard hard acrylic or a cushioned "seven-layer" system? Cushion costs a lot more and wears differently outdoors.

**Fencing.** Round posts or square? Is there a top rail? How far in from the slab edge are posts set? How high is the windscreen?

**Shade later?** If you might add a structure, is the slab priced pergola-ready with footers where columns will land?

**Schedule.** Does the bid explain cure time before coating? A slab poured in winter usually waits to be coated.

## Let the board choose what to build

Itemized pricing does something else useful. It lets the board make choices.

Maybe the surfacing and fence fit this year's budget and the extension waits. Maybe a hoop gets added later. Basketball is by far the most-used backyard and multipurpose sport, and a hoop can always be added later if it isn't in the first phase.

When everything is separate, you can build in phases without asking for a whole new bid.

## Red flags

- A bid that won't itemize.
- A slab price that's far lower than the others with no explanation of what kind of slab it is.
- Soil work quoted as a firm number before any geotech report.
- No mention of cure time or coating schedule.

None of these automatically mean a bad builder, but each one deserves a direct question.

## The bottom line

Don't compare court bids on the total. Ask all three builders to price slab, surfacing, fencing, hoops and extensions separately, then line them up and ask what's behind each number. You'll see the real differences, and your board can decide what to build now and what can wait.
