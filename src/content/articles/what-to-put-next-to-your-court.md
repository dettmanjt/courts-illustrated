A landscape architect asked us what should go right up against the court edge: grass, decomposed granite, pavers?

Here's the short answer. Treat acrylic like stucco. Keep grass, mulch and beds about 2 inches below the court surface, or put a rock border around it. Pavers can be flush. And keep fine quarter-inch DG away from the court. It's about the worst thing you can put next to one.

## Treat acrylic like stucco

You wouldn't run stucco down into the dirt. Water wicks sideways under it, and it fails from the bottom.

Acrylic court surfacing works the same way. If soil, mulch or grass sits right up against the edge, water creeps under the coating. Then it peels and delaminates.

So the rule is simple: keep the edge of the court about 2 inches above the surrounding grade.

## Your options at the edge

**Grass, mulch and planting beds.** Fine, as long as they sit about 2 inches below the court surface. That gap keeps water from wicking under the coating.

**A rock border.** A good option if you want a clean, low-maintenance edge that drains well.

**Pavers or other paved surfaces.** These can be flush with the court. They don't hold moisture against the edge the way soil does.

## Why fine DG is the worst choice

Quarter-inch decomposed granite looks great in a landscape plan. Next to a court, it causes problems.

It tracks onto the court on shoes, balls and wheels. And it's sharp enough to scratch the surface. We've seen courts that look like someone sprinkled gold dust across them.

If you love the DG look, there are better choices:

- Use half-inch-minus instead of quarter-inch.
- Or use a well-bound DG.
- Either way, set it an inch or two below the court.

## Think about water, too

The court itself sheds water. We build about 99% of our courts at a 1% slope, which is 1 inch every 10 feet, or about 3.6 inches across a 30-foot court. That water has to go somewhere.

When you're planning the landscape around a court, think about where the runoff will go so it doesn't pond against the edge or cause problems next to it. Your builder and landscape designer should talk about this early.

## A quick checklist for landscape plans

- Court edge sits about 2 inches above grass, mulch and beds.
- Rock border or pavers where you want a hard edge.
- No quarter-inch DG next to the court. If you use DG, go half-inch-minus or well-bound, an inch or two below the surface.
- A plan for where court runoff drains.

## The bottom line

Water and grit are the two things that hurt a court edge. Keep soil and plantings about 2 inches below the surface so water can't get under the acrylic, use pavers or rock where you want a hard edge, and keep fine quarter-inch DG away from the court. It's an easy detail to get right in the design and an expensive one to fix later.
