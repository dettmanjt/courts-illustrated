People ask this all the time: when can you paint the court, and why not in winter?

Short answer: a new slab needs at least 30 days to cure, and then the ground has to warm up and dry out before we coat it. So a slab poured in December usually gets coated around February or March. That doesn't mean the court sits useless. You can put up the fence, nets and even temporary lines and start playing.

## Why the slab has to cure first

Fresh concrete holds a lot of water, and it keeps changing as it cures. Once a slab is poured, it needs at least 30 days before we put acrylic on it.

Coating too early is asking for trouble. The coating system is designed to bond to cured concrete. Rushing it to hit a date is one of those decisions that saves a few weeks now and costs you a resurface later.

## Why ground temperature and moisture matter

Cure time is only part of it. After the slab cures, we still need the ground temperature up and the moisture down.

Acrylic coatings are water-based and need the right conditions to dry and bond properly. Cold ground and damp conditions work against that. Winter in our area tends to bring both.

That's why a December pour usually means a February or March coating. It isn't us being slow. It's waiting for conditions where the coating will actually last.

## What you can do while you wait

This is the part I want people to hear: the court is usable before it's painted.

While the slab waits for coating weather, we can:

- Put up the fence
- Install the nets or hoops
- Add temporary lines so you can play

Your family or your members get to use the court through the winter. Then, when conditions are right, we come back and coat it.

## Planning your project around it

If you're starting a court project in the fall, here's how I'd think about it:

- **Pour timing:** Pouring in late fall or winter is fine. Just expect coating to wait.
- **Expectations:** Tell your board, your family or your GC up front that the coating is a separate step on a separate date. That avoids a lot of frustration.
- **Use:** Decide whether you want fence and nets up first so you can play during the wait.
- **Weather days:** Any outdoor job this time of year will have some. Ask your builder how they schedule around them.

If a contractor promises to coat a brand-new slab in the middle of winter, ask them how they're handling cure time, ground temperature and moisture. You want a good answer to that question.

## The bottom line

We don't coat courts in winter because the slab needs at least 30 days to cure, and the coating needs warm, dry ground to bond. A December slab usually gets coated in February or March. In the meantime, put the fence and nets up, add temporary lines and play on it. Waiting a few weeks is how you get a surface that lasts.
